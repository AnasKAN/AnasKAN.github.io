EXERCISE 1 · THE FEATURE PYRAMID (§1.2 · §2)

Which head catches it?

YOLO11 looks at every 640 × 640 image through three grids: a fine one for small things, a coarse one for big things. Six objects from different cameras are below. For each one, pick the grid that will own it.

Work with the person next to you. Do the arithmetic together, one of you per object, then swap and check each other.

01The rule of thumb

size at 640 = √(w × h) × 640 / max(W, H)

Letterbox can shrink or grow

A 1920-wide CCTV frame is shrunk 3×, so a 30 px helmet becomes 10 px. A 416 px dataset image is enlarged 1.54×. The camera's resolution is not what the network sees.

10 px ≈ one stride-8 cell

A stride-8 cell covers 8 × 8 px. A 10 px helmet is barely more than one cell, which is why small objects are hard and why §7.1 tries imgsz=1024.

8,400 = 80² + 40² + 20²

One candidate per cell per grid. At 1024 px it is 128² + 64² + 32² = 21,504 candidates, about 2.6× the work.

A rule of thumb, not a law

YOLO11 assigns positives with task-aligned assignment, not hard size bands. But the §2.3 heatmaps show the banding is real: the bus fires on stride 32, the small workers on the fine grids.
